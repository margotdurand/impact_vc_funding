# impact_vc_funding

I examine the impact of venture capital (VC) funding on start-up valuations, comparing European and U.S. start-ups. 

Using a proprietary dataset of VC-backed companies from 2003 to 2024 we employ log-log OLS regressions with controls for company age, number of investors, fund characteristics, and industry and year fixed effects. 

I construct a panel dataset integrating firm-level, deal-level, and investor-level characteristics. 

The regressions reveal a robust and statistically significant positive relationship between VC funding and post-round valuations (β = 0.713, p < 0.001; R2 = 0.855). 

On average, a 10% increase in venture funding corresponds to a 7.1% rise in valuation, supporting the hypothesis that VC participation acts as a credible market signal that reduces information asymmetry and enhances firm value. 

Regional results show that this effect is stronger in the U.S. than in Europe, although the difference becomes statistically insignificant once firm and fund characteristics are controlled. Robustness and instrumental variable (2SLS) tests confirm the causal nature of the relationship, with IV estimates suggesting an even stronger elasticity (β = 1.160). 

Overall, the findings demonstrate that venture capital serves both as a financial catalyst and a signaling mechanism driving startup valuations. These results underscore the importance of investor reputation, syndication, and ecosystem structure in shaping valuation outcomes across venture markets.
